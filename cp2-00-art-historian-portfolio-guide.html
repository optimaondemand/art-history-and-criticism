📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A whole year of looking is behind you. What is the strongest case you can make for how your eye and your judgment have grown?
For your year-end project you assemble an Art Historian Portfolio: a curated selection of your best analysis, comparison, and creation work from both semesters, gathered into one document, with a final reflective essay on what you learned about seeing and judging art. This guide shows you how to select, organize, and reflect; the assignment that follows is where you build it.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS PROJECT YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to select my strongest analysis, comparison, and creation work from across the year.
2
I will be able to organize my work into a clear, well-presented portfolio document.
3
I will be able to write a reflective essay tracing how my looking, judgment, and writing grew.

📚 PART 1 · WHAT AN ART HISTORIAN’S PORTFOLIO IS~12 MIN
👀 LOOK BACK OVER THE YEAR
Open your OneDrive folder and scroll through everything you made this year. The cave paintings, the kouros, the cathedral, the soup can. Each piece of your work is a record of you learning to see. A portfolio gathers the best of that record and lets it speak for itself.

An art historian does not just admire art; they describe it precisely, analyze how it works, interpret its meaning, and judge it with evidence. A portfolio is the professional way to show that you can do all four, consistently, across many kinds of art. This is not a folder of everything you turned in. It is a curated selection of your strongest work, chosen on purpose and arranged to show your range and your growth.

Think of it as the case you make for yourself as a young art historian. The pieces you include are your evidence. The reflective essay at the end is your argument about what they show.

📚 PART 2 · SELECTING YOUR WORK~18 MIN

Choose work in three categories, drawing from both semesters so your portfolio shows range across time and culture:

• Analysis pieces: at least two of your four-step analyses (for example, an ancient or medieval work from Semester 1 and a modern work from Semester 2).
• Comparison pieces: at least one comparison or discussion contribution where you set two works or traditions against each other.
• Creation pieces: at least one of your studio creations (a photo of your own work), with its artist’s note.

Pick the work you are proudest of, and aim for variety: different eras, different kinds of art, different skills. You may lightly polish a piece before including it, but it should remain your own work from this year.

💬 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
As you look back, which piece of your own work first comes to mind as your strongest?
📚 PART 3 · ORGANIZING THE DOCUMENT~12 MIN

Build your portfolio as a single Word document, organized so a reader can navigate it easily. A clear structure:

• Title page: "Art Historian Portfolio," your name, and the course and year.
• Table of contents: the sections and their page numbers.
• Analysis section: your selected analyses, each with the work’s facts (and a link if the work is modern).
• Comparison section: your selected comparison piece(s).
• Creation section: photos of your own work with their artist’s notes.
• Final reflective essay: the closing argument about your growth.

📚 PART 4 · WRITING THE FINAL REFLECTION~15 MIN

The reflective essay is the heart of the portfolio. In it, you step back and make sense of the whole year. A strong reflection is honest and specific, pointing to actual pieces in your portfolio as evidence. Consider:

• What did you learn about seeing and judging art using the four-step method across the year?
• How did your eye and your writing grow from your first analysis to your last? Point to two pieces and name the difference.
• Which work or era moved you most, and why?
• What will you carry with you about how to look at any new work, including art you have never seen before?
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · THE REFLECTION MUST BE YOURS
An AI tool can produce a smooth, generic essay about "growth," but it cannot know what actually changed in you: which work surprised you, where your first analysis fell short, what you can now see that you missed in September. That honest, specific account of your own growth is exactly what this essay is graded on, and it is something only you can write. Point to your real pieces and tell the truth about them.
🧭 OPTIONAL · A VR GALLERY OF YOUR YEAR
If you would like, you may also stage your portfolio in the course virtual environment: place images of your chosen works and your own creations on the walls of a gallery space, add a short label beside each, and capture a brief walkthrough. This is optional enrichment; the Word portfolio is the required deliverable.

✏️ START HERE · IN YOUR NOTEBOOK
Before you open Word, make your selection list. Write down the analysis pieces, comparison piece(s), and creation piece(s) you will include, noting the unit each comes from and checking that you draw from both semesters. Then write one sentence naming the single biggest way your eye has grown this year. That sentence is the seed of your final reflection.

💪 WHY THIS MATTERS

You began the year unable to say much about a cave painting beyond "it is old." You end it able to describe, analyze, interpret, and judge works across thirty thousand years and to make your own. This portfolio is where you see that journey whole. An art historian is not someone who knows every date; it is someone who can stand before any work and begin to see. By now, that is you. Make a portfolio you would be proud to hand to a future teacher, a college, or your future self.
